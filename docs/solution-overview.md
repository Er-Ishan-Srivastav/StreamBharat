# StreamBharat — solution overview

## Executive summary

StreamBharat is an OTT data engineering and analytics platform built around a fictional Indian streaming service. It addresses a practical data problem: playback, billing, catalogue, advertising, search and CDN records are generated by different systems, arrive in different formats and have different reliability characteristics. Business decisions become difficult when those records cannot be reconciled into consistent, reusable facts.

The project combines:

- a PySpark / Delta Lake implementation of the original Q1–Q20 workbook;
- a Kafka event-ingestion extension with source topics, Bronze persistence, invalid-event routing and offset reconciliation;
- Silver and Gold transformations for engagement, subscriptions, advertising and finance;
- a PostgreSQL serving warehouse and Metabase dashboard layer;
- an optional Airflow DAG for date-aware orchestration.

It is best understood as a **lakehouse analytics solution with an event-driven ingestion extension**, not a production OTT application or a deployed cloud platform.

## 1. Business problem → data product

| Business problem | Data engineering response | Business-facing product |
|---|---|---|
| Playback events are duplicated, delayed or recorded on inaccurate device clocks. | Event-key deduplication, clock correction, explicit event-time handling, checkpointed ingestion and source lineage. | Clean playback facts, viewing sessions and watch-time measures. |
| Catalogue metadata changes over time and episodes belong to parent series. | Nested JSON flattening, genre/language/cast bridges, SCD Type 2 history and parent-series mapping. | Queryable content dimensions, historical catalogue snapshots and ranked title views. |
| Subscription states, retries and gateway settlements do not align one-to-one. | Event-time lifecycle derivation, seven-day payment-failure grace logic and bounded charge/settlement reconciliation. | Subscription periods, cohort retention, MRR bridge and revenue reconciliation. |
| Ad breaks and impressions can be filled, missing, orphaned or over cap. | Break/impression matching, campaign and CPM calculations, frequency checks and pacing aggregates. | Ad delivery facts, campaign performance, fill-rate and exception views. |
| CDN logs use provider-specific formats and edge identifiers. | Parser normalisation, edge/region mapping, malformed-line quarantine and rate-card joins. | Hourly usage, regional egress and delivery-cost outputs. |
| Product teams need discovery and retention signals. | Search normalisation/conversion, implicit ALS recommendation and chronologically evaluated churn scoring. | Content-gap reports, recommendations and prioritised subscriber risk scores. |
| Invalid source data and inconsistent totals can quietly reach BI. | Validation reason codes, DLQ, quarantine paths, DQ rules and published-metric reconciliation. | Auditable exceptions, data-quality gates and reconciled warehouse metrics. |

| Q9 | Audience metrics | Computes DAU/WAU/MAU, stickiness and published-metric comparisons. |
| Q10 | Content rankings | Rolls episodes to series and builds regional/national Top 10 and kids lists. |
| Q11 | Cohorts and MRR | Produces first-paid-month cohorts, retention matrices and movement bridges. |
| Q12 | Account-sharing signals | Detects repeated concurrency and multi-city patterns against plan entitlements. |
| Q13 | Advertising analytics | Measures fill, impressions, completion, CPM revenue, cap violations and campaign pacing. |
| Q14 | Performance and CDN cost | Compares baseline/AQE/salted joins and computes title/region delivery costs. |
| Q15 | Partner royalties | Allocates subscription/ad revenue by partner share, applies minimum guarantees and reconciles paise. |
| Q16 | Recommendations | Trains implicit-feedback Spark MLlib ALS and evaluates against a language-popularity baseline. |

| Q18 | Live ad pacing | Maintains impression state, frequency caps and campaign spend alerts. |

### Batch and streaming split

The finite batch orchestrator runs **Q1–Q16, Q19 and Q20** in dependency order. Q17 and Q18 are long-running streaming jobs and are deliberately launched separately. The workbook simulator's file-based streams and the Kafka extension's topic-based live interfaces are related but distinct execution paths; do not treat one as proof that the other has been validated.

### Medallion responsibilities

| Layer | Contract | Example content |
|---|---|---|
| Raw | Immutable source inputs; never edit in place. | Course JSON, JSONL, CSV and CDN files. |
| Bronze | Preserve source payload and ingestion lineage. | Kafka topic, partition, offset, key, headers, timestamps and raw value. |
| Silver | Apply explicit schemas, timestamp and unit correction, deduplication and conformance. | Clean player events, subscription SCD2 and standardised ad/CDN records. |
| Gold | Publish measures at an agreed business grain. | Watch time, active users, title rankings, campaign metrics, revenue and royalties. |
| Quality | Keep invalid records, exceptions and reconciliation results visible. | DLQ, quarantine tables, quality gates and variance reports. |

## 4. Cross-cutting engineering decisions

- **Event lineage:** Kafka source topic, partition and offset are retained so Bronze records can be reconciled with broker-retained ranges.
- **Replay-aware processing:** checkpoints, stable event identifiers and Delta merge keys make retry behaviour explicit. They do not make every source-to-sink operation globally exactly-once.
- **Schema evolution:** player payload versions are handled explicitly, including the seconds-to-milliseconds position change.
- **Time correctness:** device clock offsets, offline events, event time and server-arrival time are treated as distinct concepts.
- **Stateful business logic:** subscription SCD2, grace periods, frequency caps, session boundaries and ledger-based deduplication are represented in data rather than dashboard-only calculations.
- **Financial precision:** charge/settlement matching, partner allocations, minimum guarantees and rounding are designed for auditable reconciliation.
- **Data quality:** malformed or semantically invalid source records receive reason codes and are routed to quarantine/DLQ rather than silently discarded.
- **Separation of concerns:** transformation code writes Delta outputs; PostgreSQL serves query-ready tables/views; Metabase queries the warehouse.

## 5. Serving layer and dashboard products

PostgreSQL schema **ottdw** is the serving boundary for BI. Spark writes to staging tables and the serving code applies transactional upsert/date-slice logic. Metabase contains three collections of business-facing views:

| Dashboard | Decision area | Example questions |
|---|---|---|
| Executive OTT Performance | Audience, content and commercial performance | Daily engagement, top titles by language, subscription mix, payment-failure grace and campaign spend. |
| Streaming & Delivery Operations | Event flow and delivery quality | Stream freshness/lag, ad fill, orphan impressions, DLQ reasons, delivery exceptions and CDN costs. |
| Finance & Data Quality | Revenue integrity and controls | Settlement reconciliation, partner royalties, DQ status, churn and live pacing alerts. |

The recorded synthetic acceptance verified **3 dashboards and 20 saved SQL questions**. Optional streaming cards may return no populated data until their respective streams are run.

## 6. Validation: what is evidenced

As of 27 November 2026, the local 20-user synthetic run established the following:

- Core infrastructure smoke: 5/5 checks passed.
- Seven Kafka source feeds: 241 Bronze rows in total.
- Invalid source records: 2 records routed to DLQ (INVALID_RATING and MISSING_POSITION).
- Kafka-to-Bronze reconciliation: 22 topic-partition rows, all reconciled with zero count delta.
- Silver: 60 player events and 60 subscription SCD2 rows.
- Gold, advertising, financial and DLQ outputs passed the demo verifier's required minimum row counts.
- PostgreSQL: 12 serving tables populated and verified.
- BI: all 20 committed SQL questions executed; 3 dashboards and 20 saved questions verified.
- Engineering checks: 77 local pytest tests, Python compilation, shell syntax and Kafka/Airflow Compose validation passed.
- GitHub Actions Python and Kafka ETL contract workflows passed for the merged runtime fixes.

The synthetic run was completed by stage-by-stage recovery after runtime fixes. The corrected one-command wrapper has not yet been independently rerun on a fresh isolated set of Kafka topics, checkpoints and volumes. The acceptance data is synthetic and does not represent the full course dataset.

See [project-status-and-validation.md](project-status-and-validation.md) for detailed output counts, issue history, run IDs and limitations.

## 7. Explicit non-goals and current gaps

The current local Compose profile is a developer integration environment. It is not a production HA cluster and does not establish production-scale capacity.

Not yet evidenced by the recorded acceptance:

- full execution and reconciliation of the supplied course dataset;
- end-to-end acceptance of every Q1–Q20 business output;
- Airflow full-refresh/date-sliced DAG execution;
- continuous Q9/Q10 session and ad matching streams;
- Q17/Q18 live-stream runtime acceptance;
- complete DQ, CDN and published-board reconciliation on the original reference files;
- broker outage, checkpoint restart, late-event and alert retry drills;
- measured throughput, end-to-end latency, consumer lag and resource baselines;
- AWS deployment, IAM/network security review or production cost sizing.
